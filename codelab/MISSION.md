# Mission: Knowing When to Vibe-Code and When to Engineer with Agent Skills

## Why
AI coding agents make it effortless to generate thousands of lines of working code in minutes—and just as easy to accumulate unmaintainable **Comprehension Debt** when chat context is cleared. The goal of this Codelab is to help you master **both** modes of working with Google Antigravity (`agy`)—rapid exploratory **Vibe Coding** and disciplined **Skill-Driven Engineering** (`mattpocock/skills` + `google/skills`)—so you can consciously choose the right approach for your own projects at every stage of their lifecycle.

## Success looks like
- You can articulate the concrete trade-offs between **Baseline Vibe-Coding** (fast, low-ceremony exploration of an unfamiliar problem space) and **Skill-Driven Engineering** (`/wayfinder`, `/grilling`, `/domain-modeling`, `/prototype`, `/to-spec`, `/to-tickets`, `/implement-spec`, `/code-review`).
- You can inspect the recorded **Terminal Casts** and **Session Traces** from the Audio Electronics Inventory experiment to see how a single global rule (`AGENTS.md`) compares against composable repository skills (`SKILL.md`) across `/clear` session boundaries.
- You can run your own side-by-side experiment in `agy` on a project domain of your choice—using vibe coding to explore possibilities quickly, then switching to `/wayfinder` once you want durable documentation (`GLOSSARY.md`, `docs/adr/`, `spec.md`) and tested code for ongoing maintenance.
- You can extend a local project toward low-cost serverless production on Google Cloud (**Cloud Run** + **Cloud Firestore**) using [`google/skills`](https://github.com/google/skills) as an open-ended capstone exercise.

## Constraints
- **No mechanical copy-pasting**: You do not need to replicate the exact audio electronics inventory app built in the experiment recordings. Use the recorded runs as a field guide, and apply the workflows to your own requirements and ideas.
- **Balanced engineering judgment**: Neither approach is always right or always wrong. Treat ceremony as a dial you turn up only when ongoing maintenance, domain complexity, or production deployment justifies it.

## Out of scope
- Deep-dive Rust language syntax tutorials (Rust + HTMX + Turso is shown only as the concrete artifact produced during the recorded **Skilled Run**).
- Automated live provisioning of paid Google Cloud VMs during the lessons (cloud architecture and deployment with `google/skills` is presented as an architectural walkthrough and open-ended reader exercise).
